Your rankings, your rules

Every NBA ranking is really an argument about what matters. Is it points per game or efficiency? The box score or the plus-minus? Does a rim protector's defense count as much as a scorer's buckets? This site lets you settle it yourself: move the sliders and the list re-ranks instantly.

It covers all five positions (point guards, shooting guards, small forwards, power forwards and centers) plus head coaches, with this season's stats, updated every night during the season, and every season back to 2000-01.

New here? Pick a tab up top, then try a preset from the dropdown at the top of the filter menu (the filter button above the list, on the left, opens and closes it). Each preset ranks around a different idea of "best". Then drag a few sliders and watch the list move.

Quick start

  • Sliders set how much each stat counts. 50% is normal, 100% is double, 0% ignores it.
  • Presets load a ready-made set of sliders built around a kind of player (a floor general, a 3-and-D wing, a rim protector...); Default puts every slider back at 50%.
  • The season dropdown above the list ranks any season from 2000-01 on with the same sliders, so you can see how your formula grades another year.
  • Drag rows to rank by hand. Your order sticks until that tab's sliders change.
  • Hover any column label for the full stat name and the league average.
  • Click a name (or its card) for the player card: an overview with a skill radar, archetype and scouting report, every stat as a rank, and his seasons.
  • In the bar above the list: filter opens the filter menu, copy copies your list, info opens this panel and the gear has the settings.
  • Badges under a name mark that season's honors: the championship, the conference title, MVP, Defensive Player of the Year, All-NBA, All-Defensive, All-Star, Coach of the Year and more (see the FAQ).

How the rankings work

Every player gets a score from the stats you weigh, and the list is sorted by that score. Here's what goes into it:

Sliders and weights

Every stat is first put on the same scale: how far above or below the list's average a player is, measured in standard deviations (so 60% true shooting counts as much as 28 points a game if they're equally far from typical). That score is multiplied by its slider: at 50% a stat counts normally, at 100% twice as much, and at 0% not at all. Nothing is weighted behind the scenes, so the sliders alone decide what matters. A stat's eye switches it off entirely and hides its column. A card's eye does the same for a whole group.

Good and bad stats

Stats where lower is better (turnovers and turnover rate) count against a player. Extreme values are capped, so one fluky number can't swamp the list. A shooting percentage from too few attempts (under 25 threes or 20 free throws) shows as "-" and counts as average.

No hand-made grades

Plenty of basketball is hard to measure (defense most of all), but nothing here is a grade somebody made up. Defense is read from what can be counted: Defensive Box Plus/Minus, steal and block rates, defensive rebounding, and On-Off (how the team does with him on the floor against off it, which catches what the box score misses, noisily). The voters' opinions show up as badges instead of stats: All-Defensive teams, Defensive Player of the Year and Clutch Player of the Year sit under names and never move the ranking.

Who's on each tab

Players are listed at the position Basketball-Reference lists for their season (a "SG-PG" counts as a shooting guard). Min Games in the settings sets how many games it takes to be listed, as a share of the season so far: 10% to start, in steps of 10%, down to 1 to see everyone. The settings show the games it takes.

Support grades

The Support columns grade the situation around a player, F to A+ on a curve across the league: Teammates (how good the rest of his team was, without him: their Box Plus/Minus weighted by minutes) and Coaching (his team's coaching lift, from the Head Coaches tab).

Better support counts slightly against a player: it's credit for doing more with less, so a big season next to an All-Star cast or under a coach getting the most out of everyone counts a little less. It's a light touch (a fifth of a normal stat's strength), and the Support card's eye or the sliders turn it off.

Head coaches

The Head Coaches tab ranks every coach of the season (an interim coach has his own row, with his own games, record and playoff wins; the team's ratings are its whole season's). Its key stat is the Coaching Lift: how much better the team played than its roster's talent coming in. The talent is last season's Box Plus/Minus of the players he used, weighted by their minutes for him (a newcomer to the league counts as replacement level), turned into the net rating it predicts. A coach whose team beats that got more out of the roster; one whose team falls short got less. W vs Pt Diff is how many more (or fewer) games the team won than its point differential implies: mostly close games, where coaching and luck both play a part.

Totals and per game

Counting stats read per game to start (points, rebounds and assists per game, the way the NBA talks). Stat Base in the settings switches them to season totals or an 82-game pace. Per game and the pace rank the same; they just read differently. Win Shares and VORP are always season totals.

Ranking by hand

Drag a row by its handle to move it. A hand-dragged order stays put when you switch tabs, and is kept until you change that tab's own sliders or settings.

The player card

Click any name for his card. It has three tabs:

  • Overview: related stats rolled up into skills (scoring, efficiency, shooting, playmaking, rebounding, defense, impact and overall value), each as a percentile in the list. They make up the radar, pick his archetype (a Floor General, a 3-and-D Wing, a Stretch Five, a Rim Protector...) and fill the scouting report, strengths first.
  • Stats: every column as a rank in the list, with the list average.
  • Seasons: his career year by year, each season ranked with your sliders. Click one for that season's card.

The takes

The points under the archetype read the season in context: a small sample or missed games, how the team did with him on the floor against off it, a heavy scoring load (and whether it came efficiently), his defense, and his career so far: where he is (a promising rookie, an established star, a veteran), whether this is a breakout or a down year against his recent norm, streaks, and the skills that have defined his career. A card only looks at that season and the ones before it.

Similar seasons

Finished seasons show the three closest seasons by anyone else since 2000-01: every stat is compared by how far it stood out from its own year's league, so a 2004 season and a 2024 one line up fairly (a lot more threes go up now).

Tips & tricks

  • Reorder columns by dragging a column header within its group. Reorder groups or stats by dragging the cards and slider rows in the filter menu; the table follows.
  • Eyes switch a stat or a whole group off: the column hides and it stops counting, but its slider keeps its value.
  • Color-coded values are green above the league average and red below it. Counting stats compare per game, so missed time doesn't turn a good player red. Hover a column label for the average of what the column shows.
  • Rank tiles: a trophy for #1, green through 10, white in the middle and red for the bottom 10.
  • Copy puts your whole ranked list on the clipboard, ready to paste into a group chat.
  • The bandage marks players on the injury report. Hide them with Injured Players in the settings.
  • Min Games in the settings leaves out players who played less than a share of the season, in steps of 10%. Try 70%+ for full-season regulars, or 1 to see everyone.
  • Hover any badge under a name for the award written out.

FAQ

Pick a season from the dropdown above the list. Your sliders, eyes and column order stay as they are; the list re-ranks with that season's stats. Each season keeps its own hand-dragged order while the page is open. A season is named for both years it spans (2024-25).

That season's honors, hover one for the details. A gold trophy for the champion and a silver trophy for both conference champions (the two Finals teams), on every row from that team. Plaques for MVP, Defensive Player of the Year (DPOY), Rookie of the Year (ROY), Sixth Man (6MOY), Most Improved (MIP), Clutch Player of the Year (CPOY, from 2023), All-NBA first, second and third teams (NBA1-3, in gold, silver and bronze), All-Defensive first and second teams (DEF1-2), the All-Star team (AS) and, for coaches, Coach of the Year (COY). A traded player's trophy follows the team he finished the season with.
