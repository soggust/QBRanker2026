Your rankings, your rules

Every baseball ranking is really an argument about what matters. Is it the back of the baseball card or the advanced stats? Power or on-base? Is a shortstop's glove worth as much as a DH's bat? This site lets you settle it yourself: move the sliders and the list re-ranks instantly.

It covers every position (catchers, first, second and third base, shortstops, outfielders and designated hitters) plus starting and relief pitchers, with this season's stats, updated every night, and every season back to 2000.

New here? Pick a tab up top, then try a preset from the dropdown at the top of the filter menu (the filter button above the list, on the left, opens and closes it). Each preset ranks around a different idea of "best". Then drag a few sliders and watch the list move.

Quick start

  • Sliders set how much each stat counts. 50% is normal, 100% is double, 0% ignores it.
  • Presets load a ready-made set of sliders built around a kind of player (a slugger, an ace, a closer...); Default puts every slider back at 50%.
  • The season dropdown above the list ranks any year from 2000 on with the same sliders, so you can see how your formula grades another season.
  • Drag rows to rank by hand. Your order sticks until that tab's sliders change.
  • Hover any column label for the full stat name and the league average.
  • Click a name (or its card) for the player card: an overview with a skill radar, archetype and scouting report, every stat as a rank, and his seasons.
  • In the bar above the list: filter opens the filter menu, copy copies your list, info opens this panel and the gear has the settings.
  • Badges under a name mark that season's honors: the World Series, the pennant, MVP, Cy Young, Rookie of the Year, Gold Glove, Silver Slugger and All-Star (see the FAQ).

How the rankings work

Every player gets a score from the stats you weigh, and the list is sorted by that score. Here's what goes into it:

Sliders and weights

Every stat is first put on the same scale: how far above or below the list's average a player is, measured in standard deviations (so a .300 average counts as much as 40 home runs if they're equally far from typical). That score is multiplied by its slider: at 50% a stat counts normally, at 100% twice as much, and at 0% not at all. Nothing is weighted behind the scenes, so the sliders alone decide what matters. A stat's eye switches it off entirely and hides its column. A card's eye does the same for a whole group.

Good and bad stats

Stats where lower is better (a hitter's strikeout rate; a pitcher's ERA, WHIP, walks and home runs allowed...) count against a player. Extreme values are capped, so one fluky number can't swamp the list. A Statcast stat a player has no reading for counts as average. A stat nobody has that season (Statcast starts in 2015) is left out altogether.

Who's on each tab

Hitters are listed at their primary position (left, center and right field together as outfielders). Pitchers who started at least half their games are starters; the rest are relievers. A two-way player like Ohtani shows on both sides. Min PA in the settings sets how much playing time it takes to be listed, as a share of the most plate appearances on the tab (batters faced, for pitchers): 10% to start, in steps of 10%, down to 1 to see everyone. It grows with the season, and the settings show the plate appearances it takes.

Support grades

The Support columns grade the situation around a player, F to A+ on a curve across the league. Hitters get Lineup (how good the rest of his team's lineup was, without him: more runners on and more hitters behind him mean more RBI and runs) and Stadium (how friendly his home park is to hitters). Pitchers get Defense (his team's fielding runs behind him) and Stadium (how friendly his park is to pitchers). Park factors are Baseball Savant's, a three-year average.

Better support counts slightly against a player: it's credit for doing more with less, so a great season in Coors Field or in front of a Gold Glove infield counts a little less. It's a light touch (a fifth of a normal stat's strength), and the Support card's eye or the sliders turn it off. (wRC+, FIP and WAR already adjust for the park on their own.)

Totals and per game

Stat Base in the settings switches counting stats between season totals, per game (fairer to anyone who missed time) and a full-season pace: 162 games for hitters, 32 starts for starters and 65 appearances for relievers. Per game and the pace rank the same; they just read differently.

Small samples

Rate stats aren't adjusted for how much playing time they come from. Raise Min PA in the settings to leave out players with too little.

Ranking by hand

Drag a row by its handle to move it. A hand-dragged order stays put when you switch tabs, and is kept until you change that tab's own sliders or settings.

The player card

Click any name for his card. It has three tabs:

  • Overview: related stats rolled up into skills (contact, power, plate discipline, speed, defense... or for pitchers, missing bats, control, run prevention, limiting damage), each as a percentile in the list. They make up the radar, pick his archetype (a Slugger, a Table Setter, an Ace, a Lockdown Closer...) and fill the scouting report, strengths first.
  • Stats: every column as a rank in the list, with the list average.
  • Seasons: his career year by year, each season ranked with your sliders. Click one for that season's card.

The takes

The points under the archetype read the season in context: luck (a hitter's wOBA against his expected wOBA from quality of contact; a pitcher's ERA against his FIP), a real or costly glove, a small sample, and his career so far: where he is (a promising rookie, an established star, a veteran), whether this is a breakout or a down year against his recent norm, streaks, and the skills that have defined his career. A card only looks at that season and the ones before it.

Similar seasons

Finished seasons show the three closest seasons by anyone else since 2000: every stat is compared by how far it stood out from its own year's league, so a 2004 season and a 2024 one line up fairly.

Tips & tricks

  • Reorder columns by dragging a column header within its group. Reorder groups or stats by dragging the cards and slider rows in the filter menu; the table follows.
  • Eyes switch a stat or a whole group off: the column hides and it stops counting, but its slider keeps its value.
  • Color-coded values are green above the league average and red below it. Counting stats compare per game, so missed time doesn't turn a good player red. Hover a column label for the average of what the column shows.
  • Rank tiles: a trophy for #1, green through 10, white in the middle and red for the bottom 10.
  • Copy puts your whole ranked list on the clipboard, ready to paste into a group chat.
  • The bandage marks players on the injured list. Hide them with Injured Players in the settings.
  • Min PA in the settings leaves out players with less than a share of the season's playing time (plate appearances; pitchers: batters faced), in steps of 10%. Try 50%+ for everyday players only, or 1 to see everyone.
  • Hover any badge under a name for the award written out.

FAQ

How do past seasons work?

Pick a year from the season dropdown above the list. Your sliders, eyes and column order stay as they are; the list re-ranks with that year's stats. Each season keeps its own hand-dragged order while the page is open. Statcast stats (xwOBA, barrels, hard-hit rate, sprint speed, whiffs, xERA) start in 2015, so before that their columns and sliders are left out.

What are the badges under names?

That season's honors, hover one for the details. A gold trophy for the World Series champion and a silver flag for both pennant winners, on every row from that team. Plaques for MVP, the Cy Young (CY), Rookie of the Year (ROY), the Gold Glove (GG), the Silver Slugger (SS) and the All-Star team (AS), from either league. A traded player's trophy follows the team he finished the season with.

Why is my guy ranked so low?

The ranking only reflects the sliders. Try a preset that fits his game, or switch Stat Base to Per Game or Full-Season Pace if he missed time. And if you still disagree, drag him where he belongs.

What is WAR?

Wins Above Replacement: how many wins a player added over a freely available fill-in, rolling hitting, baserunning, defense and position (or pitching) into one number. 2 is a solid regular, 5 an All-Star, 8+ an MVP-type season. It's one slider like any other.

What does "-" mean?

The stat isn't available for that player, usually a Statcast stat with too few batted balls or pitches to measure. Missing Statcast stats count as league average.

When do the stats update?

The current season updates every night, once the day's games are final. Finished seasons don't change.

Is my ranking saved?

No. Everything resets when you reload the page: sliders, drags, column changes, the settings menu and the season (back to the current one). Copy your list if you want to keep it.

A player I'm looking for isn't listed.

He may be under the Min PA setting (plate appearances, or batters faced for a pitcher; lower it to see more), listed at a different position (his primary one), or on the injured list with Injured Players turned off. At 1, everyone who batted or pitched is listed.
