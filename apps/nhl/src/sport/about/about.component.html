Your rankings, your rules

Every NHL ranking is really an argument about what matters. Is it points, or driving play at 5-on-5? Goals, or the chances behind them? Does a shutdown defenseman count as much as one who runs the power play? This site lets you settle it yourself: move the sliders and the list re-ranks instantly.

It covers teams, centers, left wings, right wings, defensemen, goalies and head coaches, with this season's stats, updated every night during the season, and every season back to 2008-09, playoffs included.

New here? Pick a tab up top, then try a preset from the dropdown at the top of the filter menu (the filter button above the list, on the left, opens and closes it). Each preset ranks around a different idea of "best". Then drag a few sliders and watch the list move.

Quick start

  • Sliders set how much each stat counts. 50% is normal, 100% is double, 0% ignores it.
  • Presets load a ready-made set of sliders built around a kind of player (a sniper, a playmaker, a shutdown defenseman, a shot-stopping goalie...); Default puts every slider back at 50%.
  • The season dropdown above the list ranks any season from 2008-09 on with the same sliders, so you can see how your formula grades another year.
  • Drag rows to rank by hand. Your order sticks until that tab's sliders change.
  • Hover any column label for the full stat name and the league average.
  • Click a name (or its card) for the player card: an overview with a skill radar, archetype and scouting report, every stat as a rank, his games and his seasons.
  • Click a row anywhere but the name to pick it, then compare to put up to four side by side (see Compare).
  • In the bar above the list: filter opens the filter menu, copy copies your list, share makes a link to it, compare opens Compare, info opens this panel and the gear has the settings.
  • Badges under a name mark that season's honors: the Stanley Cup, the conference titles, the Hart, Vezina, Norris, Calder, Selke and more (see the FAQ).

How the rankings work

Every player gets a score from the stats you weigh, and the list is sorted by that score. Here's what goes into it:

Sliders and weights

Every stat is first put on the same scale: how far above or below the list's average a player is, measured in standard deviations (so a 55% expected-goals share counts as much as 40 goals if they're equally far from typical). That score is multiplied by its slider: at 50% a stat counts normally, at 100% twice as much, and at 0% not at all. Nothing is weighted behind the scenes, so the sliders alone decide what matters. A stat's eye switches it off entirely and hides its column. A card's eye does the same for a whole group.

Good and bad stats

Stats where lower is better (giveaways, penalty minutes, goals-against average) count against a player. Extreme values are capped, so one fluky number can't swamp the list. A rate from too small a sample (shooting percentage under 20 shots, faceoffs under 50, save percentage under 50 shots) shows as "-" and counts as average.

No hand-made grades

A lot of hockey is hard to count (defense most of all), but nothing here is a grade somebody made up. Play away from the puck is read from the shots and chances: xGF % and CF % (his team's share of the expected goals and shot attempts while he's on the ice at 5-on-5) and Rel xGF % (how much better his team does with him on than off). The voters' opinions show up as badges instead of stats: the Selke, Norris and Hart sit under names and never move the ranking.

Who's on each tab

Players are listed at the position the NHL lists for them that season. Min Games in the settings sets how many games it takes to be listed, as a share of the season so far: 10% to start, in steps of 10%, down to 1 to see everyone. The settings show the games it takes.

Support grades

The Support columns grade the situation around a player, F to A+ on a curve across the league. Skaters get Linemates: how good his team was without him, its expected-goals share at 5-on-5 with him on the bench. Goalies get Defense: the team in front of him, read from the quality of the shots he faced (expected goals against per 60 minutes; A+ is the stingiest). Everyone gets Coaching: his team's Coaching Lift from the Head Coaches tab.

Better support counts slightly against a player: it's credit for doing more with less, so a big season behind a stingy defense or on a stacked roster counts a little less. It's a light touch (a fifth of a normal stat's strength), and the Support card's eye or the sliders turn it off.

Goalies

A goalie's save percentage depends on the shots he faces. GSAx (goals saved above expected) fixes that: every shot gets the odds an average goalie stops it, from where and how it was taken, and GSAx is the goals that adds up to minus the goals he actually allowed. +20 is a Vezina-type season. His record is his own decisions (wins, losses and overtime losses).

Head coaches

Each coach is credited with the games he actually coached, so a mid-season firing splits the season: his own record, playoff wins (the coach who finished it), goal differential per game, and his points over goal differential (close games, overtime and shootouts). The team's season goes on every coach it had: its 5-on-5 expected-goal share, power play and penalty kill, and its league ranks in goals for and against.

Coaching Lift is the headline: how much better the team controlled play at 5-on-5 than its roster predicted. The prediction is the skaters' own expected-goal share last season, weighted by their ice time for the team this season and pulled a quarter of the way to average. +3 means the team played three points of expected-goal share above its talent: credit for getting more out of a roster, not for having a good one.

Totals and per game

Counting stats read as season totals to start (goals, assists and points, the way the NHL talks). Stat Base in the settings switches them to per game or an 82-game pace. Per game and the pace rank the same; they just read differently. Game Score and GSAx are always season totals.

The Teams tab

The first tab ranks the teams: record, recent results, playoff wins, goal differential, offense and defense ranks, the power play and penalty kill, expected-goal and shot-attempt share, the goalies' GSAx, points over goal differential and PDO. Then Forwards, Defense and Goalies: each group graded from your own rankings on the player tabs, weighted by ice time (a goalie by his starts). Re-rank the defensemen and every team's Defense grade here follows.

Playoffs

Length in the settings switches the stats between the regular Season, the Playoffs or Both together (rates worked out over those games), for any season from 2008-09 on. It's greyed out until a season's playoffs start. In the playoffs an overtime loss is simply a loss. A playoffs or both card leaves out its career takes and similar seasons, which would weigh a few games against whole regular seasons.

Ranking by hand

Drag a row by its handle to move it. A hand-dragged order stays put when you switch tabs, and is kept until you change that tab's own sliders or settings.

The player card

Click any name for his card (a team's or a coach's works the same way). Its tabs:

  • Overview: related stats rolled up into skills (goal scoring, finishing, playmaking, shot volume, driving play, defense, physicality and overall value; faceoffs for centers; a goalie's shot stopping, save percentage, big saves, workload and winning), each as a percentile in the list. They make up the radar, pick his archetype (a Pure Sniper, a Play Driver, a Shutdown Defenseman, a Brick Wall...) and fill the scouting report, strengths first.
  • Team (teams and coaches): the top unit drawn on a rink, every line, pair and goalie, the coaching staff and everyone who played. Names open their own cards.
  • Stats: every column as a rank in the list, with the list average. The Recent squares open their games.
  • Game Log: that season's games, a chart of the key stat by game, then a row each (the playoffs set apart). A team's goals for and against (PF and PA) sit on little scoreboard tiles, the winner's lit green and the loser's red. The date opens the game, the opponent its team's card. This season's card also lists the games still to come, with the puck drop, the TV and the betting line.
  • Career (History for teams): every season he's had, each ranked with your sliders. Click one for that season's card.

The compare button at the top of the card takes that season into Compare. Hover any chart (the radar, a skill's bar, a stat tile, the game log's bars) for whose it is, what it is and the number.

The takes

The points under the archetype read the season in context: a small sample, finishing that ran hot or cold against his chances (often luck that evens out), whether his team tilted the ice with him on, a power-play-heavy season, a huge minutes load, a goalie stealing (or leaking) goals and the defense in front of him, and his career so far: where he is (a promising rookie, an established star, a veteran), whether this is a breakout or a down year against his recent norm, streaks, and the skills that have defined his career. A card only looks at that season and the ones before it.

Similar seasons

Finished seasons show the three closest seasons by anyone else since 2008-09 (click the panel to open it, and one for its card): every stat is compared by how far it stood out from its own year's league, so seasons from different eras of scoring line up fairly.

A team's vs Position

A team's Overview has a vs Position panel: what it allowed to opposing forwards and defensemen, points and shots a game, and vs Avg, the points against what those same skaters averaged in their other games. The rank comes from that: holding a top line under its usual output reads as good defense, and #1 is the stingiest. A take under the archetype says when defensemen get an unusual share of the points against it. It's there to read, not to rank: nothing in it moves the list.

The game view

A game opened from the card has the scoreboard, then the summary, box score, plays, shot chart and fantasy points. A finished game with video has Highlights, playing right on the board; one still to come has a preview with the injury report.

Compare

Put up to four seasons side by side: any players, any years, any tabs. Three ways in:

  • Pick rows in the list: click a row anywhere but the name (the name opens the card), and again to let it go (from the keyboard: C on a name; Escape lets go of them all). The compare button in the bar counts the picks; click it to compare them.
  • The compare button at the top of a player card takes that season in.
  • Open compare with nothing picked and search anyone the site has had, any season. A name adds his best season; the season under each name switches it to another of his years.

Each season is read against its own league: that season's list on his tab, ranked with your sliders, just as his card reads it. So a 2010-11 season and a 2024-25 one meet on even ground: where each stood among his own peers.

Share, at the top of Compare, copies a link that opens the same comparison, ranked with the same sliders.

Overview

Everyone's skills on one radar and as bars (percentiles, 100 the top). Edges lists where one is clearly the best of them (10 percentile points or more over the next best); How Alike gives each pair a score, 100% being the same skill profile.

Stats

The grid's columns side by side, each value tinted as the grid tints it, with a bar for where it stood that season. Each column's leader is lit, and over each player is how many columns he leads. A line under the names sums it up: who leads the most columns, and the most skills (with three or four, each one's count of columns led under it).

Career

Each one's career arc: every season's rank in its league (with the default sliders), the compared season ringed. The dropdown lines them up by Career Year (first season, second...) or by Season.

Different positions

A center and a defenseman can go side by side, each read among his own position. Only what's the same by name lines up: a skill only when both have it, and a column both tabs have goes to the bigger number, since percentiles among different positions don't say who did more.

Tips & tricks

  • Reorder columns by dragging a column header within its group. Reorder groups or stats by dragging the cards and slider rows in the filter menu; the table follows.
  • Eyes switch a stat or a whole group off: the column hides and it stops counting, but its slider keeps its value.
  • Color-coded values are green above the league average and red below it. Counting stats compare per game, so missed time doesn't turn a good player red. Hover a column label for the average of what the column shows.
  • Rank tiles: the Stanley Cup for #1, green through 10, white in the middle and red for the bottom 10.
  • Copy puts your whole ranked list on the clipboard, ready to paste into a group chat.
  • Share copies a link that opens your list exactly as it looks: the tab, the season, your sliders, eyes, settings, dragged rows and columns. On a phone it opens the share sheet.
  • Scroll sideways through the columns and the rank and team card stay pinned at the left, so every stat keeps its owner beside it.
  • Hover a chart (the radar, a bar, a career dot, a game's win probability) for whose it is, what it is and the number.
  • Rookies Only in the settings lists just the rookies, by the Calder Trophy's rule: the race for rookie of the year.
  • Min Games in the settings leaves out players who played less than a share of the season, in steps of 10%. Try 70%+ for full-season regulars, or 1 to see everyone.
  • Hover any badge under a name for the award written out.

FAQ

The ranking only reflects the sliders. Try a preset that fits his game, or check Min Games if he missed time. And if you still disagree, drag him where he belongs.

That season's honors, hover one for the details. A gold cup for the Stanley Cup champion and a silver cup for both conference champions (the two Final teams), on every row from that team. Gold plaques for the voted trophies: the Hart (MVP), Vezina (goalie), Norris (defenseman), Calder (rookie), Selke (defensive forward), the Conn Smythe (playoff MVP) and the Lindsay (the players' MVP). Silver plaques for the scoring titles won on the stat sheet: the Rocket Richard (goals) and the Art Ross (points). A traded player's cup follows the team he finished the season with.

GSAx is a goalie's goals saved above what an average goalie would have on the same shots. Each is one slider like any other.
