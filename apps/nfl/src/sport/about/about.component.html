Your rankings, your rules

Every NFL ranking is really an argument about what matters. Is it wins or stats? Volume or efficiency? Did he carry the team, or did the team carry him? This site lets you settle it yourself: move the sliders and the list re-ranks instantly.

It covers quarterbacks, running backs, wide receivers, tight ends, offensive lines, kickers, punters, defenses and head coaches, all with this season's stats, updated every night, plus every season back to 2000.

New here? Pick a tab up top, then try a preset from the dropdown at the top of the filter menu (the filter button above the list, on the left, opens and closes it). Each preset ranks around a different idea of "best". Then drag a few sliders and watch the list move.

Quick start

  • Sliders set how much each stat counts. 50% is normal, 100% is double, 0% ignores it.
  • Presets load a ready-made set of sliders built around a style of play; Default puts every slider back at 50%.
  • The season dropdown above the list ranks any year from 2000 on with the same sliders, so you can see how your formula grades another season.
  • Drag rows to rank by hand. Your order sticks until that tab's sliders change.
  • Hover any column label for the full stat name and the league average.
  • In the bar above the list: filter opens the filter menu, copy copies your list, info opens this panel and the gear has the settings.
  • Badges under a name mark that season's honors: the Super Bowl, conference titles, MVP and the other major awards, All-Pro and Pro Bowl picks (see the FAQ).

How the rankings work

Every player (or team, or coach) gets a score from the stats you weigh, and the list is sorted by that score. Here's what goes into it:

Sliders and weights

Every stat is first put on the same scale: how far above or below the list's average a player is, measured in standard deviations (so 72% vs. 65% completion counts as much as 10 vs. 5 touchdowns if they're equally far from typical). That score is multiplied by its slider: at 50% a stat counts normally, at 100% twice as much, and at 0% not at all. Nothing is weighted behind the scenes, so the sliders alone decide what matters. A stat's eye switches it off entirely and hides its column. A card's eye does the same for a whole group.

Good and bad stats

Stats where lower is better (turnovers, sacks allowed, points allowed, stuffed runs...) count against a player. Extreme values are capped, so one fluky number (say, 100% on two chances) can't swamp the list, and a stat a player has no data for counts as the list's worst, or as average when "-" just means no chances yet. A stat nobody has that season (it wasn't tracked yet) is left out altogether.

Small samples

Stats aren't adjusted for how many games they come from, so a single stat sorts the list exactly by its numbers, and a fill-in with one great game can rank high on rate stats. Raise Min Games in the settings to leave out players with too few games.

Totals, breakdowns and per game

Paired stats like yards and touchdowns have a total slider with a breakdown underneath (click the label to open it). The total scales both parts. Stat Totals in the settings switches counting stats between season totals, per game (fairer to anyone who missed games) and a 17-game pace: per game over a full 17-game season, so a 12-game year or a 16-game era reads like a full modern season. Per game and 17-game pace rank the same; they just read differently. A QB's games are his starts plus any relief appearance with real snaps (10+ dropbacks and runs), so a brief cameo doesn't halve his per-game numbers.

Ranking by hand

Drag a row by its handle to move it. A hand-dragged order stays put when you switch tabs, and is kept until you change that tab's own sliders or settings.

Grades and support

Some columns are letter grades from F to A+. They describe the situation around a player rather than the player himself: the offensive line, the weapons, the coaching, the defense.

Every grade is on a curve: teams are ranked by their score, the best grades an A+, the worst an F, and the rest spread evenly in between. So there's always a full range, whatever the scores are.

Why better support counts against a player

It's credit for doing more with less. A QB putting up big numbers behind a bad line, throwing to average receivers, is more impressive than one with every advantage. So better support slightly lowers a player's score. It's a light touch, and you can turn it off with the Support card's eye or by setting the sliders to 0%.

The exception is the QB page's Responsibility grade: how much the offense runs through the QB. Carrying the offense counts for him.

How Responsibility is worked out

  1. Only the games he led count: those where he had the most dropbacks for his team.
  2. Play share: his dropbacks (passes, sacks, scrambles) and designed runs, out of all his team's offensive plays in those games. Kneel-downs and spikes are left out.
  3. Yard share: his passing plus rushing yards, out of all his team's yards in those games.
  4. Each share is compared across all the QBs (how far above or below the average he is), the two count equally, and the QBs are ranked on the result: the top one an A+, the bottom one an F.
  5. Early in the season that's blended with a preseason grade, leaning on the season more with every game (see below), then curved again so the grades always run the full A+ to F.
  6. In the ranking it counts at the same light strength as the other support grades, but in the QB's favor. Its slider sets how much.

The tabs grade each other

  • QB Play comes from the QB tab, weighted by each QB's starts for the team.
  • RB Play comes from the RB tab, weighted by each back's carries.
  • Weapons comes from the RB, WR and TE tabs: each player's spot weighted by his targets (and carries for backs), with receivers counting 50%, backs 30% and tight ends 20%.
  • O-Line comes from the Offensive Lines tab.
  • Defense comes from the Defenses tab.
  • Coaching comes from the Head Coaches tab.

Grades follow each tab's current order, including your drags: move the Packers' line to #1 on the Offensive Lines tab and every Packers QB and RB gets a better O-Line grade. Disagree with a team grade? Re-rank the tab it comes from.

Preseason vs. this season

Early on, a few games isn't much to go on, so Weapons, O-Line and Coaching grades blend a preseason grade (set by AI before the season; see Data & Credits) with this season's results. The season counts for about 17% after 1 game, 35% after 3, 56% after 6, 80% after 10, and completely from game 14 on. The blended scores are then curved like the rest. First-year head coaches start from a neutral C, since there's no track record to grade. A finished season uses its full-season results.

Tips & tricks

  • Reorder columns by dragging a column header within its group. Reorder groups or stats by dragging the cards and slider rows in the filter menu; the table follows.
  • Eyes switch a stat or a whole group off: the column hides and it stops counting, but its slider keeps its value.
  • Color-coded values are green above the league average and red below it. Counting stats compare per game, so missed games don't turn a good player red. Hover a column label for the average of what the column shows.
  • Rank tiles: a trophy for #1, green through 10, white in the middle and red for the bottom 10.
  • Copy puts your whole ranked list on the clipboard, ready to paste into a group chat.
  • The bandage marks players ESPN lists as Out, Doubtful or on IR (in a past season, those who finished it on IR). Hide them with Injured Players in the settings.
  • Turn off Garbage Time Stats to leave out plays run once the game was decided (win probability under 10% or over 90%). Stats padded in blowouts shrink.
  • Unit Ranks in the settings sets what the head coaches' Off and Def Rank columns rank on: points or yards per game.
  • Combine Rush/Pass Stats shows yards and touchdowns as single totals, leaving more room for other columns.
  • Min Games in the settings leaves out players who played less than a share of the season so far, in steps of 10% (the settings show the games it takes). Early on, 10% is a game; set it to 1 to see everyone.
  • Hover any badge under a name for the award written out.

FAQ

How do past seasons work?

Pick a year from the season dropdown above the list. Your sliders, eyes and column order stay as they are; the list re-ranks with that year's stats. Each season keeps its own hand-dragged order while the page is open. A finished season uses its full-season results for every grade (no preseason blend), and the bandage marks players who finished it on injured reserve. Stats that weren't tracked yet that year are left out, columns and sliders both: tracking stats like separation and time to throw start in 2016, CPOE, YAC and air yards in 2006, and 2003 to 2008 have no targets (so no target share or catch rate either).

What are the badges under names?

That season's honors, hover one for the details. A gold Lombardi for the Super Bowl champion and a silver cup for both conference champions, on every row from that team. Gold plaques for the major awards: MVP, Offensive Player (OPOY) and Rookie (OROY) of the Year, Comeback Player (CPOY), SB MVP, Coach of the Year (COY) on the coach, and the defensive awards (DPOY, DROY) on the winner's defense. Silver AP1 / AP2 for the AP's first and second All-Pro teams, and bronze PB for the Pro Bowl (players voted in, plus alternates called up as replacements). Badges show for finished seasons, back to 2000; a fullback or defender only gets one on a tab he's on.

Why is my guy ranked so low?

The ranking only reflects the sliders. Try a preset that fits his game, check whether a support grade is pulling him down, or switch Stat Totals to Per Game or 17-Game Pace if he missed time. And if you still disagree, drag him where he belongs.

Why does a better supporting cast hurt a player?

It's credit for doing more with less (see Grades & Support). It's a small effect and you can switch it off with the Support card's eye.

What is the QB's Responsibility grade?

How much of the offense runs through the QB. In each game he led (the most dropbacks for his team), it measures his share of the team's plays (his dropbacks, scrambles and designed runs, leaving out kneels and spikes) and his share of the team's yards, passing plus rushing. Those two are combined and compared across all the QBs, blended with a preseason grade early in the year (the season takes over as games are played), and curved: the QB who carries his offense the most is an A+, the one most carried by it an F.

Unlike the other support grades, it counts for the QB (carrying the offense is to his credit), at the same light strength as the rest. Turn its slider up or down, or to 0% to leave it out.

How is the Weapons grade worked out?

From the RB, WR and TE tabs. Each player gets a grade from his spot in his own tab (#1 is an A+, last is an F), and a team's grade at each position averages its players by how much they're used: targets for receivers and tight ends, carries plus targets for backs (catches in 2003 to 2008, which have no targets). The three positions are then combined, with receivers counting 50%, running backs 30% and tight ends 20%, since it's mostly about who the QB is throwing to. Like the other team grades, it starts from a preseason grade and leans on this season's rankings more with every game played. Finally the 32 teams are ranked by that score and graded on a curve: the best group of weapons is an A+, the worst an F.

How do I change a grade I disagree with?

Team grades (QB Play, RB Play, Weapons, O-Line, Defense, Coaching) come from the other tabs' rankings, so re-rank the tab they come from: drag a team's line up the Offensive Lines tab, or a receiver up the WR tab, and their grades follow everywhere. The QB page's Responsibility grade is worked out from each QB's share of his team's plays and yards; if you think it counts too much or too little, turn its slider up or down.

What does "-" mean?

The stat isn't available for that player yet, usually because a data source doesn't cover him or he hasn't had the chance (no kicks from 50+, no short-yardage runs). Missing rate stats like 4th Down Go % count as league average; most others count as the worst value.

When do the stats update?

The current season updates every night, early in the morning Eastern time, once the day's games are final and the data sources have caught up. Monday night games show up the following morning. Finished seasons don't change.

Is my ranking saved?

No. Everything resets when you reload the page: sliders, drags, column changes, the settings menu and the season (back to the current one). Copy your list if you want to keep it.

Why don't some stats change with Garbage Time Stats off?

Only stats built play by play can be filtered: EPA, success rate, CPOE and most defense and O-line stats. Season totals like yards and touchdowns, and tracking stats like separation, stay the same.

Why do early-season rankings jump around?

Small samples. One big game can swing a rate stat a lot in September (raise Min Games in the settings to leave out players with too few). The team grades lean on preseason grades early and shift to this season's results as games are played.

A QB I'm looking for isn't listed.

The QB tab lists quarterbacks who started a game that season. If he's there but hidden, he may be injured with Injured Players turned off, or under the Min Games setting.

Data & credits

Where the numbers come from

  • ESPN: game results, QB box score stats (passer rating is worked out from them), the injury report and head coaches.
  • nflverse: play-by-play (EPA, success rate, CPOE, win probability), season stats and fantasy points, snap counts, Next Gen Stats tracking data and Pro Football Reference's charting (pressures, broken tackles, drops, bad throws), plus schedules, coaches and Super Bowl results for past seasons.
  • Wikipedia: the AP All-Pro teams and Pro Bowl rosters behind the AP1 / AP2 / PB badges. The season awards (MVP, OPOY and the rest) are the AP's.
  • Preseason grades (team Weapons, O-Line and Coaching, and each QB's Responsibility) are set by AI before the season, then blended out as games are played. A first-year head coach has no track record, so he starts at a neutral C.
