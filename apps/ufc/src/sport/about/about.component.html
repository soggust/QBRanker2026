Your rankings, your rules

Every fight ranking is really an argument about what matters. The record, or who it came against? Knockout power, or never getting hit? Wrestling, or the striking battle? This site lets you settle it yourself: move the sliders and the list re-ranks instantly.

It covers every active fighter in the big promotions (a fight in the UFC, PFL, Bellator or Rizin in the last two years), updated every night, rated on every fight of his career across ten promotions since 1997. Each fighter is listed in one division (the one he fights in now), ranked on his whole career; the P4P tab ranks everyone against his own division's best and lists the top 30. UFC Fighters Only in the settings narrows the lists to the UFC's roster. The dropdown above the list picks Current MMA, Current WMMA, All-Time MMA or All-Time WMMA: all-time adds the retired legends, and WMMA switches the tabs to the women's.

New here? Pick a division up top, then try a preset from the dropdown at the top of the filter menu (the filter button above the list, on the left, opens and closes it). Each preset ranks around a different idea of "best". Then drag a few sliders and watch the list move.

Quick start

  • Sliders set how much each stat counts. 50% is normal, 100% is double, 0% ignores it.
  • Presets load a ready-made set of sliders built around a kind of fighter (a knockout artist, a wrestler, a submission artist, the best resume...); Default puts every slider back at 50%.
  • Head to head: a fighter ranked right below someone he beat in their latest meeting (the last three years) moves above him.
  • Drag rows to rank by hand. Your order sticks until that division's sliders change.
  • Hover any column label for the full stat name and the division average.
  • Click a name for the fighter card: an overview with a skill radar, archetype and scouting report, every stat as a rank, and his fights.
  • In the bar above the list: filter opens the filter menu, copy copies your list, info opens this panel and the gear has the settings.
  • Badges under a name: the belt for each division's champion, and a plaque for the pound-for-pound top 15 (UFC.com's rankings).

How the rankings work

Every fighter gets a score from the stats you weigh, and the list is sorted by that score. Here's what goes into it:

Sliders and weights

Every stat is first put on the same scale: how far above or below the division's average a fighter is, measured in standard deviations (so 5 significant strikes a minute counts as much as 3 takedowns per 15 minutes if they're equally far from typical). That score is multiplied by its slider: at 50% a stat counts normally, at 100% twice as much, and at 0% not at all. Nothing is weighted behind the scenes. A stat's eye switches it off entirely and hides its column.

Good and bad stats

Stats where lower is better (strikes absorbed, times knocked down, times finished) count against a fighter. Extreme values are capped, so one fluky number can't swamp the list. A rate from too small a sample (a strike accuracy under 50 attempts, a takedown accuracy under 5) shows as "-" and counts as average.

The rating

At the heart of it is a rating built from every pro fight since 1997 in the UFC, PFL, Bellator, Rizin, PRIDE, Strikeforce, WEC, KSW, Cage Warriors and LFA, in the order they happened. Everyone starts level (a newcomer to the UFC a little higher, as the UFC signs proven fighters), and each result moves both fighters by how surprising it was: beating a highly rated opponent is worth far more than beating a low one, and a finish counts fully where a split decision counts for less. Each rating carries an uncertainty, wide for a newcomer and growing while a fighter sits out, and is shown cautiously (less half of it), so a 3-0 prospect doesn't leap over a proven contender. Tested on every fight since 2020, it picks the winner more often than a plain Elo rating.

Current and all-time

The current lists are about who is best now: today's Rating leads (with UFC.com's own rank, for UFC fighters), and career totals (title wins, finishes, quality wins) don't count. The all-time lists lead with Career Pts, built like Fight Matrix's: every month since 1997, each division's active fighters are ranked by the rating, and the top 15 earn points, the most for #1 and fewer down the list (full points only when the division was deep). A long reign at the top adds up; a brief spike doesn't. His Peak rating counts too.

The career, not a season

Fighters fight two or three times a year, so a season says little: everything here is his whole career, in every division (the striking and grappling stats from the UFC and the PFL, where they're kept; a fighter without them isn't marked down for it). The rates are per minute of fight time (striking) or per 15 minutes (knockdowns, takedowns, submission attempts), so a short night and a 25-minute war count fairly. What he absorbs comes from his opponents' numbers in the same fights.

Who's in each division

A fighter is listed in one division: the one he fights in now, or for a retired fighter the one he earned the most career points in. His stats and record are still his whole career. Min Fights in the settings sets how many pro fights (in the promotions covered) it takes to be listed: 6 to start, in steps of 2, down to 1 to see everyone.

Small samples

A fighter four fights into his career can have perfect numbers that don't mean much yet. So each rate (accuracy, defense, strikes per minute, the record) counts in the ranking in proportion to the fights behind it: four fights count half, twelve three-quarters, a long career nearly all of it. The columns show his real numbers either way.

Strength of schedule

The rating already weighs whom he beat. Best Win (his best opponent's rating going in), Opp Rating (his opponents' average) and Quality Wins (wins over the top tenth) show it on their own, so a strong record against a soft schedule is weighed against a lesser one against the best.

Ranking by hand

Drag a row by its handle to move it. A hand-dragged order stays put when you switch divisions, and is kept until you change that division's own sliders or settings.

The fighter card

Click any name for his card. It has three tabs:

  • Overview: related stats rolled up into skills (striking output, accuracy and defense, knockout power, wrestling, takedown defense, grappling, durability and winning), each as a percentile in the division. They make up the radar, pick his archetype (a Knockout Artist, a Smothering Wrestler, a Counter Striker, a Submission Specialist...) and fill the scouting report, strengths first.
  • Stats: every column as a rank in the division, with the division average.
  • Fights: his fights in the promotions covered, newest first: the result, the opponent, the event, and how it ended (a knockout, a submission, or the kind of decision).

The takes

The points under the archetype read his career in context: a win or losing streak, how he wins (a finisher, or one who goes to the cards), whether he's ever been finished or has chin questions, whether he wins the striking battle, his quality wins, and his whole pro record.

FAQ

Why is my guy ranked so low?

The ranking only reflects the sliders. Try a preset that fits his style, or check Min Fights if he's new. And if you still disagree, drag him where he belongs.

Why careers and not seasons?

A fighter has two or three fights a year: a season is too small to rank on. His whole career gives the rates enough fight time to mean something, and the rating keeps it current: recent wins and losses move it, and it grows less certain while he sits out.

What's "significant" about a significant strike?

It's the official count of the strikes that matter: everything at distance, and the power shots in the clinch and on the ground (not the little pitter-patter ones). It's what the UFC's own stats use.

Who counts as active?

Anyone with a fight in the UFC, PFL, Bellator or Rizin in the last two years. A fighter who hasn't fought there in two years moves to the all-time lists (with 6+ fights in the big promotions).

What does "-" mean?

Too small a sample: under 15 minutes of fight time for a rate, or too few attempts for an accuracy. Or not kept at all (the striking and grappling stats outside the UFC and PFL). A stat he doesn't have is left out of his score, not counted against him.

When do the stats update?

Every night: the night after an event, its fights are in.

Is my ranking saved?

No. Everything resets when you reload the page: sliders, drags, column changes and the settings menu. Copy your list if you want to keep it.
